## What it is good at

Claude is particularly effective for long source material, structured thinking and editing where tone matters as much as accuracy.

It works well as a second point of view: give it the original context, ask it to find gaps, then compare its answer with your own judgment.

## Where it earns its place

Use it for:

- working through long documents and project specifications;
- turning rough notes into clear business writing;
- identifying assumptions, risks and missing information;
- creating first drafts that need a restrained, natural voice;
- reviewing a plan before it becomes execution.

## The H² rule

Use two models when the decision has consequences.

Different reasoning styles uncover different blind spots. The goal is not to collect answers — it is to make the final decision stronger.

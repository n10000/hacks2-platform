## The short version

Zapier is a practical way to stop repetitive information from getting stuck between familiar tools. It is a sensible first automation platform when the job is clear: route a new lead, create a task after a form is submitted, notify the right person or keep two records aligned.

Its strength is breadth and an approachable starting point. The best early Zaps remove one repeated manual handoff rather than trying to redesign the whole business at once.

## Where it earns its place

Use Zapier when you need to:

- connect widely used SaaS tools without writing code;
- turn a form, email or calendar event into a predictable next action;
- notify a person when a lead, task or exception needs attention;
- test a valuable automation quickly before investing in a bigger system;
- start from an existing template and adapt it to a real business process.

## A practical starting setup

Choose one task that happens often and has a simple definition of success. A good first version might turn a qualified website enquiry into a CRM contact and a follow-up task.

1. Keep the trigger and final action visible to the owner of the process.
2. Pass only the information the next person actually needs.
3. Test it with ordinary, incomplete and duplicate submissions.
4. Review the first month of runs before adding more steps.

## The H² rule

Use automation to create a reliable next action.

If the workflow needs several exceptions, unclear approval rules or a complex decision, simplify the process first. A short automation that people trust is more valuable than a clever one nobody can safely change.

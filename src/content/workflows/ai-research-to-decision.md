Good research is not the same as collecting links.

The purpose is to make a better decision with a visible trail of evidence. AI is valuable here because it can help structure the work, compare sources and expose unanswered questions.

## Define the decision first

Begin with one sentence:

> We need to decide whether, why or how to do this.

Then define what would change the answer. Price, technical compatibility, legal risk, customer demand, speed of implementation or expected return are all valid decision factors.

Without this boundary, research expands forever.

## Create an evidence map

Collect sources into three groups:

- primary sources: official documentation, original data and direct statements;
- supporting sources: credible analysis and specialist reporting;
- signals: reviews, community discussion and early indicators.

Ask AI to extract claims from every source, but keep the original link beside each claim. A good system never hides where important information came from.

## Separate facts from judgment

Create three columns in the decision brief:

1. What we know.
2. What we infer.
3. What remains uncertain.

This sounds simple, but it prevents confident language from turning incomplete research into a false conclusion.

## Produce the one-page brief

Finish with:

- the recommendation;
- the strongest evidence;
- the largest risks;
- the next action;
- the date when the decision should be revisited.

The outcome is not a perfect answer. It is a decision that can survive scrutiny and improve when new evidence appears.

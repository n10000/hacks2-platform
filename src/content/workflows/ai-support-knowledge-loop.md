Support conversations are product research in plain language.

Every repeated question points to something that is unclear: the product, the onboarding, the pricing, the documentation or the expectation set before purchase.

## Capture the real wording

Do not summarise too early. Save the customer's actual wording along with the issue type and resolution.

The language customers use is often more valuable than internal terminology. It shows how they think about the problem.

## Cluster recurring questions

Once a week, group similar requests. Ask AI to identify:

- repeated problems;
- questions that take too long to answer;
- confusing steps;
- requests that should become documentation;
- signals that a product feature is missing.

Review the groups manually. AI is useful for pattern detection, but only the team can judge importance.

## Build response assets

For common issues, create:

- a short direct answer;
- a step-by-step version;
- a reusable email or chat reply;
- a help article;
- an internal escalation rule.

Keep the tone natural. Automation should remove repeated typing, not empathy.

## Close the loop

Track whether the same question returns after the article or reply template is introduced.

If it does, the issue may not be the wording. It may be the underlying experience.

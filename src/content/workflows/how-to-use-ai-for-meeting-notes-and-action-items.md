Meeting notes fail in two predictable ways: nobody writes them, or they become a long transcript that nobody uses. The useful middle ground is a short record of what was decided, what remains open and who will do what next.

AI is excellent at turning a transcript or rough notes into a first draft. It is not present in the room, and it cannot reliably distinguish a tentative idea from a commitment. That distinction stays with the people who attended.

## Decide the output before the meeting starts

Every recurring meeting should have a fixed note format. Keep it short enough to scan before the next conversation:

1. Purpose and attendees.
2. Decisions made.
3. Action items with one owner and a due date.
4. Open questions or risks.
5. Links to source documents.

If an item has no owner, it is not an action item. If it has no realistic date, call it a follow-up question instead of pretending it is a plan.

## Collect a reliable source

Use the meeting agenda, a transcript where appropriate and any shared notes. Tell attendees when a meeting is being recorded or transcribed, and follow the privacy rules that apply to your organisation and customers.

Do not send confidential client material to a tool whose data controls you have not reviewed. For sensitive conversations, use only approved tools or take manual notes.

## Ask AI to separate certainty from discussion

Give the model the source material and a disciplined instruction:

> Create meeting notes using this structure: decisions, action items, open questions and risks. For each action item, list the owner and due date only if explicitly stated. Quote uncertain items under “Needs confirmation.” Do not invent commitments, attendees or deadlines.

This phrasing matters. A clean-looking summary can create problems if it silently promotes a suggestion into a decision.

## Run a two-minute human review

Before sharing the notes, check the items with consequences:

| Item | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Decision | Was it actually agreed, or merely discussed? |
| Owner | Did that person accept responsibility? |
| Deadline | Was a date said aloud, or is it a guess? |
| Client commitment | Does the wording match what was promised? |
| Sensitive detail | Should it be in a shared record at all? |

The meeting owner should make corrections, then send the summary while the context is fresh. A good target is within one business day.

## Put actions where work happens

Notes are not a task system. Move approved action items into the project board, CRM or shared task list your team already uses. Link back to the meeting note if context will matter later.

This is the point where many AI note systems break: they produce a nice document but leave people to retype the tasks. Keep the handoff small and consistent.

## Close the loop at the next meeting

Start the next session by checking the previous action list. Mark completed work, update dates and explain what changed. Over time, this turns notes into operational memory rather than a folder of forgotten summaries.

## The rule

Let AI make the record easier to create. Let humans decide what the record means.

Most client friction begins before delivery.

A request such as “make it modern” or “we need more leads” contains a real need, but not enough information to build the right solution. AI can speed up clarification — it should never replace it.

## Capture the raw request

Keep the original message intact. Then ask AI to produce:

- the likely business goal;
- open questions;
- constraints already mentioned;
- possible interpretations;
- risks of starting too early.

Use this as an internal preparation document, not as an automatic response.

## Run a structured discovery pass

Confirm five things with the client:

1. Who is the outcome for?
2. What should change after the work is done?
3. What is already working?
4. What is fixed and cannot change?
5. How will success be measured?

These questions turn taste into criteria.

## Convert it into a delivery brief

The approved brief should include scope, exclusions, milestones, required client inputs and a clear review process.

AI can draft this quickly, but you must verify every commitment. A polished sentence is still a commitment if a client reads it that way.

## Use review gates

Do not present everything at the end.

Create small decision points: direction, structure, first implementation, final quality check. This protects both sides from expensive surprises.

The best client workflow feels calm. Everyone knows what is being decided, what happens next and what is outside the current scope.
